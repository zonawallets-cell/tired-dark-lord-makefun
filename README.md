A 100-word villain monologue for make.fun's "The Tired Dark Lord" bounty: a dark lord four hundred years into his reign greets the hero who has come to end him — and keeps putting the duel off until tomorrow. The tiredness lives in the pauses, the lost thread and the small deflections, never in a plain statement; the villain keeps his scheme (the eclipse ritual needs one more night) and his history (the last hero came on a Wednesday). 101 words, 568 characters, pure Python standard library.

> Ah. The hero. Right on time. What day is it? Tuesday? The prophecy said Tuesday. Well. Sit down, then. Not there. That's the good chair. The one that doesn't... never mind. You've come to end me, yes? The eclipse, the sword, the whole performance. I've run this empire four hundred years, and nobody tells you about the paperwork. The requisitions. The goblins unionised. The last one came on a Wednesday. Look, the ritual needs one more night. And honestly, so do I. Kill me tomorrow. Same time. I'll leave the gate unlocked. Just let me finish this cup of tea first.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

Writes the monologue to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (default `/output/entry.txt`), enforcing the gate: UTF-8, 1–600 characters, about 100 words (90–110), and no outright announcement of tiredness.
